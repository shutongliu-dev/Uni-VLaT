Bringing whole-body touch to pretrained robot policies.
Learning how contact, body state, and the scene evolve together.

01 / The idea

Touch as a physical anchor.

Vision describes the scene. Touch reveals the interaction.

Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies provide reusable visual, linguistic, and action priors. Contact-rich whole-body control remains challenging when physical interaction is weakly observed or visually occluded. Uni-VLaT adapts these policies with distributed whole-body tactile sensing.

A topology-aware spatial encoder and a causal four-frame history turn distributed measurements into tactile tokens. These tokens interact with vision-language, robot-state, and action features inside the policy. During training, a multimodal joint-embedding predictive objective uses this tactile-anchored context to predict future tactile, proprioceptive, and visual representations.

The resulting tactile-conditioned policy generates whole-body motion for a Unitree G1. The future-prediction heads are used only during training.

02 / Method

Sense contact. Learn its consequences.

Adapt a pretrained policy through tactile integration and multimodal predictive supervision.

01

Encode whole-body touch

Region-wise embeddings capture contact across eight body regions. Axial pooling reduces sensitivity to sleeve rotation, while a four-frame temporal module captures recent contact history.

02

Connect touch to the task

Tactile, proprioceptive, and action tokens interact through self-attention. Vision and language enter through cross-attention in the pretrained policy’s DiT trunk.

03

Predict interaction dynamics

Three predictors use the contextualized tactile features to estimate four future steps of tactile, body-state, and visual latents in their own representation spaces.

At deployment

Tactile-conditioned VLA → 64-dimensional motion tokens → SONIC whole-body controller

Predictive heads and target encoders are omitted during execution.
